Chapter 1 · Low Water

What the tide leaves behind

An essay in two tides, written on the harbor wall.

Before dawn the harbor empties like a drawn breath. The boats that were floating at midnight are sitting on their keels by four, leaning into one another, their mooring lines gone slack and draped across the mud. Nothing has been taken away. The water has only stepped back far enough for you to see what was always there.

I come down most mornings to watch it happen. There is a particular quiet at low water that has nothing to do with silence: gulls work the flats, the stream in the channel keeps talking to itself, and somewhere a halyard knocks against a mast in a rhythm nobody set. But the big sound, the sea itself, has gone out of the room.

What surprises me every time is how much structure the water had been hiding. Sandbars with the shape of fingerprints. A line of old pilings from a pier nobody remembers building. Mussel beds so dense they read as a single dark coat. At high water all of it is simply "the harbor." At low water it is a map, and you can walk on it, if you know when to leave.

That is the part people forget. The tide is not a mood; it is a schedule. It will come back at the minute the table says, and it does not negotiate with anyone standing too far out on the flats.

So I read the table the night before the way other people read the weather: when the low comes, how low it goes, how long I have before the channel closes behind me.

Flat content, tilted outline

The flap's outline is projected in perspective, so it grows taller toward its free edge. The content inside is drawn flat at its resting size and never transforms. The gap between the two is dark glass, and that's where the shadow at the top and bottom comes from.

Blur grows away from the hinge

Following the open-source iphone-duo shader: blur is max × lift × distance^1.35 and darkening reaches black at the free edge. CSS can't vary blur across one element, so the flap stacks five copies at rising blur and crossfades them with masks.

What shows when

Until 90°, the flap shows B. Past 90° you're looking at its other side, so it shows C. D sits under the flap and is always sharp. Fire it from code:
